	\item Next, the school counselor was curious  whether  the average student IQ in her school is higher than the average IQ score (100) among all the schools in the country.\\ 
	\noindent Using the same sample, conduct the appropriate hypothesis test with $\alpha=0.05$.
	
	\section*{Answers For Question 1.2}
	
Hypothesis testing will begin with two statements:

\textbf{Null hypothesis is:} the average student's IQ is higher than 100 among all

schools in the country.

 HO greater 100

\textbf{Alternative hypothesis is:} the average student's IQ is not higher than 100 
among all schools in the country.

 HA less than 100

This will be at one-sided test because we are looking at if something is or is not the value of the null. 

The test statistic is calculated to determine the how far the estimate is from the null hypothesis. 

\underline{\textbf{Test = estimate - null value / SE}}

T-statistic will be used due to the small population size (i.e 25 data points) and because this is a one-sided test. 

The test  will look at y (data sampling) and the degrees of freedom. With this we can observe if the we can reject the null hypothesis of HO greater than 100  through the p-value outcome.	

\lstinputlisting[language=R, firstline=135, lastline=137, breaklines=true]{PS01_EL.R}

\textbf{Final Answer for 1.2: We failed to reject the null at the standard significance rate of 0.05 because the p-value is greater than the standard used. Thus, we cannot conclude the average IQ of the counselor's school is greater than the country's IQ scores.}
